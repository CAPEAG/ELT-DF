Heading and variation

Low wind only. GPS gives ground track, not heading. In a crosswind the nose points into the wind by the crab angle, so track and heading differ (about 11° with a 20 knot crosswind at 100 knots). The Becker measures from the nose, so every bearing would be off by that amount. Use this only in calm conditions, and read heading from the DG or HSI otherwise.

Offline maps use USGS topo, which is public domain and covers the United States. When a connection is available the map keeps loading finer detail from the internet past the stored zoom level.
