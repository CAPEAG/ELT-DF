DF settings

Heading and variation

Low wind only. GPS gives ground track, not heading. In a crosswind the nose points into the wind by the crab angle, so track and heading differ (about 11° with a 20 knot crosswind at 100 knots). The Becker measures from the nose, so every bearing would be off by that amount. Use this only in calm conditions, and read heading from the DG or HSI otherwise.

FREQ
Tap the right side of a knob to turn it up and the left side to turn it down. FREQ steps through the 15 preprogrammed channels (121.500 through 406.040, including the marine and 406 sub-channels); the unit only shows a bearing when it is tuned to the exact frequency the beacon is transmitting on, so a 406.025 beacon is silent on 406.028. SQL sets the squelch level (the marker on the signal bar); readings below it are muted. VOL turns the beacon audio on and sets its level: the 121.5 sweep tone gets louder and cleaner as you close on the beacon and hides in the hiss when the signal is weak, and 406 gives a short burst warble. Tap VOL once to enable sound (the iPad requires a touch before audio can start). Clr resets the last-signal timer.
Fly
Aircraft not placed.
Log a reading whenever you like on 121.5, or during the three seconds the bearing is displayed after a 406 burst. Each one records the aircraft position and heading with the exact bearing the Becker is showing, draws the line on the map, and adds a fix to the DF tab, where the crossings and the estimate build up just as they would on a real sortie. The plotted line uses the heading reference and variation setting from DF settings, so a wrong variation there shows up as a wrong line here, the same as in the aircraft. The aircraft can also be dragged to a new position on the map at any time. Hold the pad left or right to bank; the further you push, the steeper the bank, up to 30°. Turn rate and radius follow bank angle and airspeed, and the aircraft rolls in and out over a couple of seconds. Release to roll level.
